"Rainbow", "laser", "pillar", "iridescent": suppliers and designers use many words for holographic effects, and the same word can mean different things to different people. This glossary explains the terms you will meet when ordering holographic paperboard or film.

Base effects

Bright silver (mirror)

A plain metallized surface with no holographic structure. It reflects like a mirror and shows no rainbow colours.

Matte silver

A metallized surface with a soft, satin finish. It looks premium and reduces glare, which helps when the carton carries fine print.

Rainbow holographic

Also called "laser" or "seamless rainbow". A microscopic structure splits light into rainbow colours that move as you tilt the pack. It usually has no visible repeat or design.

Pillar (light beam)

A holographic effect made of vertical or radiating bands of light. As the pack moves, beams of colour appear to sweep across it. Some suppliers call it "light pillar" or "beam".

Pearl

A soft, pastel shimmer instead of strong rainbow colours. It suits cosmetics and personal-care packaging where a gentler look is wanted.

Patterned holographics

Patterned holographic materials carry a designed image or texture in the holographic layer itself: geometric shapes, flowing lines, fine textures or motifs. The pattern is made once as a master and then reproduced across the roll or sheet.

  • Geometric: repeating shapes such as diamonds, hexagons or facets.
  • Fluid: flowing, organic movement, such as waves or swirls.
  • Texture: fine all-over structures, such as frost, glitter or crystal effects.
  • Lines and beams: stripes, rays and light-beam designs.
  • Motifs: recognisable images or brand elements.

A ready-made pattern is the fastest route. A custom pattern, for example one with a brand logo built into the holographic layer, takes longer to set up but makes the pack harder to copy.

Other terms you may hear

Iridescent

Often used for a clear or pastel film whose colour shifts with angle. It is usually softer than a full rainbow holographic.

Registered holographic

The holographic image is positioned to line up with the printed design on every carton, rather than running all over. It needs tighter production control.

Seamless

A holographic pattern with no visible join lines across the web, so cartons can be cut from any position.

How to order the right effect

  • Send a reference photo or a physical sample of the look you want.
  • Say what the material is for (carton, rigid box wrap, label, lamination film).
  • Ask for a sample of the exact pattern on the exact substrate. Screens and photos cannot show how a holographic effect moves.
