Metallized and holographic paperboard gives packaging a strong shelf presence, but its surface behaves very differently from coated paper. The ink sits on top instead of soaking in. These are the points printers check to get good results.

1. Choose inks made for non-absorbent surfaces

Conventional sheetfed inks rely partly on absorption into the paper to set. On a metallized surface that does not happen, so drying is slow and set-off is likely. Use inks designed for non-absorbent substrates, either oxidation-drying inks formulated for foil and film board or fast-curing inks with matching dryers on the press. Ask your ink supplier for a product recommended for metallized board.

2. Check surface tension before the run

Ink adhesion depends on the surface energy of the board. Ask your board supplier for the surface tension value and test it with dyne pens on arrival. Store the board wrapped and away from heat, and use it within the recommended period.

3. Plan the white underprint

Process colours printed straight onto silver look metallic and transparent, which is often the effect you want. For areas that should look solid and opaque, such as text panels, barcodes and photos, print an opaque white first. Leaving areas without white is how designers keep the metallic shine where it matters.

4. Press settings

  • Run a little less ink and water than on coated paper, and keep the fountain solution balanced. Excess water causes poor adhesion and emulsification on non-absorbent stock.
  • Use anti-set-off spray powder carefully and keep pile heights low until the ink has fully dried or cured.
  • Allow enough drying time before back-printing, varnishing or converting.

5. Protect the print

A suitable overprint varnish or coating improves rub resistance, which matters for cartons that travel through long supply chains. Test the varnish for adhesion on the exact ink and board combination.

Always run a test

Every combination of board, ink and press behaves slightly differently. Request a sample quantity of the exact board for a press trial before committing to a production order.
